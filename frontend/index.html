      How this system works

      A rep names a prospect, and somebody has to decide what is worth saying to them and whether it is true. This is what happens in between.

      The model reads and writes. The code decides. Which hook wins is arithmetic, not a judgement call, so the same evidence always picks the same hook and a rep can audit the margin.

      1. The problem

      An SDR working a list of 200 prospects cannot spend twenty minutes on each. So they don't: the company name goes in a template and the reply rate reflects it. But the signal is there — a funding round, a new finance hire, a product launch, a regulatory filing. The work being automated here is finding it, judging whether it matters to this particular person, and turning it into two sentences worth reading.

      Three questions have to be answered before a word is written, and no single source answers any of them: is this person still there, is there anything real to say, and is it safe and recent enough to say it?

      Three kinds of answer, not one

      A draft Every factual sentence traces to a retrieved source. A rep reads it and sends it.
      Work for a human Nothing citable, or the record is wrong, or the name is ambiguous. The process says which, and stops.
      Nothing at all Every available hook was a crisis story. No email is drafted, deliberately.

      A process that returns nothing on 3 of 10 prospects and is right every time it does return something is worth more to a rep than one that always returns something.

      2. The nine stages

      Every prospect takes the same nine stages in the same order. Watch them happen on the page — each one says what it is doing, then what it found.

      A failure is then attributable to one stage rather than to "the system". Five of them can end a run before a draft exists: the identity check halts outright, and the harvest, interpretation, screen and scoring stages each stop if the thing they are looking for is not there. Everything after a stop is marked skipped, not failed, because the work was deliberately not done.

      3. Why the identity check runs first

      Drafting on a bad record is not merely wasted work — it is the specific mistake that burns an account. So the person-level lookups run before the full company harvest, and a confident "they left" or "there are two of them" stops the run there.

      The probe searches unscoped for joining and departure language, which reliably surfaces unrelated namesakes, so a halt is held to a high bar: a confident verdict that cites a specific retrieved source. Anything weaker degrades to a warning and the run continues on company-level hooks. Telling a rep their CRM is wrong is expensive, and being wrong about it is worse.

      4. How the hook is chosen

      The model's job in stage 4 is narrow: turn retrieved documents into typed claims, each one carrying the source ids it was read from. A claim that cites no retrieved id is discarded rather than kept — which is what makes an invented citation impossible rather than merely unlikely.

      What survives is then scored on five components out of a hundred, and the arithmetic picks the winner. Role relevance is the component that does the interesting work: the same funding round is worth 25 points to a finance title and 17 to an engineering one.

      Below the floor — 55 for a company-level hook, 62 for a person-level one — nothing is drafted at all. The full table, component by component, is on the page and in the scoring stage of every run.

      5. What stops it inventing things

      Writing is the one stage where a model produces prose a human will read, so it is the one stage whose output is audited rather than trusted. Every sentence in the draft is checked back against the evidence that was retrieved; an unsupported sentence triggers a rewrite, and a second failure holds the run for manual research instead of sending something plausible.

      The same applies to the safety screen: sensitivity is never a score, it is a veto. A layoff story is allowed to score on its merits precisely so that the veto is visibly the thing that removes it, rather than the ranking quietly doing it and looking like a guardrail.

      6. What this does not do

      • It never sends. There is no mail credential anywhere in the system. Approving a draft records that a human cleared it.
      • It does not score the person's seniority or fit. That is list-building, a different job with different inputs.
      • It does not write more than one hook. One permitted fact, one short email. A draft that references three things reads as research, not as a note.
      • It does not retry a dead connector indefinitely. A throttled or failing source marks its stage as degraded and the run continues with what it has — only a condition that makes the output untrustworthy stops the process.
